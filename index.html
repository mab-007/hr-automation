The state of play

Most AI projects don't fail on the model. They fail on ownership.

Gartner expects 40% of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by 2027. Only 23% of organisations have scaled an agent deployment at all. The pattern behind the failures is remarkably consistent.

✗Nobody owns it

A pilot gets built by whoever was free. It works in a demo, then drifts, then nobody knows who maintains it.

✗The data boundary was never defined

Legal asks where client data goes. There's no good answer. The project quietly stops.

✗Nothing was measured

No before-and-after numbers, so there's no case for a second phase.

We settle all three before writing a line of code.

Commercials

How engagements are structured.

Every engagement is scoped from the audit, so the numbers come out of your shortlist rather than a price list. The shape never changes:

  • A fixed-fee audit

    Priced up front and credited against the build if you proceed. You keep the written shortlist either way.

  • A one-time build fee

    Fixed and scoped from that shortlist. Changes to scope are a change order in writing, not a conversation at the end.

  • A variable component

    Tied to actual model usage and passed through transparently, with a monthly cap agreed in advance so it never surprises you.

  • Source code purchase — optional

    Priced up front, at the start, and never a renegotiation later. You decide whether to exercise it, and when.

  • Ongoing engineering service — optional

    Priced separately and cancellable. Not bundled into the code purchase and not a condition of it.
