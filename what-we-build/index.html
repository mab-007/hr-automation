What we build

The work that eats your team's week.

Six patterns cover most of what we're asked to build. Yours is probably a variation of one of them. For each one: what your team does by hand today, what we build instead, what we measure, and which decisions we deliberately leave with a person.

01 · Research & data collection

Your analysts are a very expensive copy-paste layer.

Market data, company profiles, filings, pricing, hiring signals — gathered, verified and structured, with a citation on every line.

The manual reality

An analyst gets a list of 200 target companies on Monday. By Wednesday they've done sixty: opening each website, hunting for headcount, checking a filing, pasting it into a spreadsheet, then doing it again. The work needs no judgement and takes two days. On Thursday the list changes.

What we build

An agent that works the list: pulls from the sources you already trust, cross-checks each figure against a second source, and writes the result into your own schema. Where it can't verify something it says so instead of guessing — which is the difference between a research tool and a liability.

What gets measured

  • Analyst hours per profile, before and after
  • Turnaround from request to delivered list
  • Error rate found in spot-checks
  • Share of fields verified against two sources

Where the human stays

The analyst still owns the conclusion. Nothing goes to a client or into a model's recommendation without a person reading it, and anything the agent flagged as unverified is surfaced at the top rather than buried.

02 · Document & report generation

The deliverable you produce for the fortieth time.

Reports, decks, memos, proposals and SOWs, drafted in your firm's structure and voice from your own data.

The manual reality

Every proposal starts as last quarter's proposal. Someone opens the most recent one, strips the client name, rewrites four sections, forgets to update a figure in the appendix, and a partner catches it at 11pm. The structure never changes; only the content does.

What we build

A generator that reads your own template library and data, assembles the first draft in the right structure, and pulls live figures from the system of record instead of the last document. Your voice, because it's trained on your deliverables and not on the internet's idea of a consulting report.

What gets measured

  • Hours from brief to reviewable first draft
  • Number of revision rounds before sign-off
  • Factual corrections caught in review
  • Proposals out per week, per person

Where the human stays

It produces a draft, never a send. The recommendation, the pricing and the commitment are written or confirmed by the person whose name is on the document.

03 · Screening & triage

Four hundred applications and one afternoon.

High-volume inbound scored against your criteria and ranked, with a written rationale behind every position.

The manual reality

Résumés, RFPs, leads, tickets, invoices, claims — it doesn't matter which. The volume arrives faster than anyone can read it, so the first sixty get real attention and the rest get thirty seconds each. Nobody can tell you why number two hundred was rejected, because nobody really read it.

What we build

Scoring against your rubric, not a generic one, with a written justification per item that a person can disagree with. Everything arrives ranked, with the reasoning attached and the borderline cases pushed to the top of the queue rather than the bottom.

What gets measured

  • Hours per hundred items screened
  • Time from arrival to decision
  • Agreement rate between the score and the reviewer
  • Share of the pile that got a real read (target: all of it)

Where the human stays

Every rejection. The system ranks and explains; a person decides. This isn't a courtesy — an automated rejection you can't justify is a legal exposure, and a consulting firm's brand is on every candidate experience.

04 · Scheduling & coordination

Eleven emails to book one meeting.

The back-and-forth that quietly consumes a coordinator's entire day.

The manual reality

Three partners, two time zones, one candidate, and a coordinator with six calendars open. Offer three slots, two get taken by client work, start again. Then someone reschedules on the morning and the whole sequence repeats. This is the single most complained-about job in most firms.

What we build

An agent that holds the constraints — seniority, panel composition, time zones, buffer rules, who must not be double-booked — finds the overlap, offers it, books it, and sends the brief. When someone drops out it re-solves rather than escalating to a human.

What gets measured

  • Turnaround from "ready to schedule" to booked
  • Coordinator hours per interview or meeting
  • Reschedules handled without human touch
  • Drop-off caused by scheduling delay

Where the human stays

Who sits on the panel, and any exception that affects a client commitment. The agent never moves a client meeting to solve an internal one.

05 · Back-office operations

The month-end work nobody wants to own.

Invoice and expense processing, timesheet chasing, vendor onboarding, compliance checklists, reconciliation.

The manual reality

It lands on whoever is least able to say no. Invoices get keyed in twice, timesheets get chased by Slack message, and the compliance checklist is a spreadsheet somebody remembers to open. None of it is anyone's actual job, which is exactly why it slips.

What we build

Extraction and matching against your PO and project structure, coding to the right cost centre, and a queue that surfaces only the exceptions. Chasing happens on a schedule without anyone feeling like the person who nags.

What gets measured

  • Days to close the month
  • Cost per invoice or expense processed
  • Exception rate, and how many need a human
  • Timesheet compliance by the deadline

Where the human stays

Anything that moves money or creates an obligation. Payment, write-off and vendor approval stay a person's decision, with the full trail attached so the decision takes seconds instead of an investigation.

06 · Client-facing reporting

The same report, every Monday, forever.

Recurring reports and dashboards assembled from your systems on a schedule, in your template, ready for a partner's review.

The manual reality

Someone spends Friday afternoon, or Monday morning, pulling the same numbers out of the same four systems into the same deck. It is the most reliably repeated task in the firm and the least defensible use of a senior person's time — and if they're on leave, the client notices.

What we build

A scheduled assembly from your systems of record into the client's template, with the movements since last period called out rather than left for the reader to spot. It lands before the stand-up, in draft, every time — including when the person who usually builds it is away.

What gets measured

  • Hours per report cycle
  • On-time delivery rate
  • Corrections raised by the client
  • Reports a single person can carry

Where the human stays

The commentary and the send. A partner reads it and decides what the numbers mean before a client ever sees them.

Featured use case

AI hiring automation, worked all the way through.

Hiring is where several of the patterns above land at once, so it's the example we can be most concrete about. This is one use case, not our whole business — the same four workflows have equivalents in every function that runs on people's hours.

Résumé screening with competency scoring

Scored against your own consulting rubric — structured reasoning, domain depth, communication — with a written justification per candidate. The output is a ranked shortlist a hiring partner can argue with, which is the only kind worth having.

Interview scheduling across partner calendars

The highest-frustration, lowest-risk, most visible win in the whole function. Panel composition, seniority rules, time zones and buffers held as constraints, re-solved automatically when someone drops.

JD and role-kit generation

A hiring manager's five-line brief becomes a job description, screening questions, a scoring rubric and outreach copy — in your firm's voice, ready to edit rather than ready to send.

Hiring-funnel reporting

Assembled from the ATS on a schedule: stage conversion, time-to-shortlist, offer-to-join drop-off, agency spend. The numbers that tell you whether any of the above is working.

✓
A human approves every rejection. Without exception.

The system scores, ranks and explains. It never sends a rejection, and it never makes an offer. This is both the ethical line and the practical one: an automated rejection you cannot justify in writing is a legal exposure, and in a firm that hires from a small number of campuses, every candidate experience is your brand. Candidate PII stays inside your tenancy throughout — the architecture is here.
